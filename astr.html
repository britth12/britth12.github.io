KEY HR DIAGRAM FACTS:
  • •X-axis: Temperature (decreasing L→R!)
  • •Y-axis: Luminosity (increasing bottom→top)
  • •Main Seq: diagonal band upper-left → lower-right. Stars spend ~90% of life here.
  • •Mass on MS: high mass = upper-left, low mass = lower-right
  • •Giants: upper-right (cool but very luminous → huge radius)
  • •White Dwarfs: lower-left (hot but dim → tiny radius)
  • •Constant R lines run diagonally (↗).
  • •L = 4πR²σT⁴ — explains all positions
  • •MS Turnoff: In cluster, where stars leave MS. Higher turnoff = younger cluster.
  • •Massive stars make many shallow H-R loops as they cycle through fusion stages
  • •Protostar path: starts upper-right, moves left & down to MS

Ch 15 — Surveying the Stars (~20%)
  • Parallax: d(pc) = 1/p(arcsec). Larger p → closer star. Only works for nearby stars.
  • Apparent brightness = how bright from Earth. Luminosity = total energy output (intrinsic).
  • Inverse Square Law: b = L / (4πd²) — double distance → ¼ brightness
  • Luminosity-Radius-Temp: L = 4πR²σT⁴
    — double T → L × 16 (T⁴); double R → L × 4 (R²)
  • Spectral Types: OBAFGKM (see table)
  • MS Lifetime: t ≈ M/L — since L∝M³·⁵, lifetime ∝ M⁻²·⁵. More massive = much shorter life!
  • Cluster ages: Find main-seq turnoff point. Higher turnoff = younger; lower = older.

Ch 16 — Star Birth (~20%)
  • Molecular clouds: Cold (~10–30 K), dense regions of gas & dust. Sites of star formation.
  • What causes collapse? Gravity overcomes thermal pressure. Triggers: shockwaves from supernovae, cloud collisions, spiral arm compression.
  • 3 processes during collapse:
    • Heats up — gravitational PE → thermal KE
    • Spins up — conservation of angular momentum (shrink → spin faster)
    • Flattens out — forms protostellar disk (angular mom. prevents collapse along equator)
  • On the HR diagram: Protostar starts upper-right (cool, luminous, large), moves LEFT and DOWN toward MS as it contracts and heats up.
  • Turn-on time: High mass → MS fast (~millions yr). Low mass → slow (~billions yr).
  • Mass distribution: Low-mass stars far more common than high-mass (IMF).
  • Low-mass limit (~0.08 M☉): Below this, degeneracy pressure halts contraction → brown dwarf
  • High-mass limit (~150+ M☉): Radiation pressure blows material away.
Key Equations & Relationships
  • d = 1/p — parallax (pc & arcsec)
  • b = L / (4πd²) — inverse square law
  • L = 4πR²σT⁴ — Stefan-Boltzmann
  • V_esc = √(2GM/R)
    • Derived from ½mv² = GMm/r
    • ↑M → ↑V_esc; ↑R → ↓V_esc
    • Double M → V_esc × √2
    • Double R → V_esc / √2
    • Does NOT depend on mass of escaping object!
  • lifetime ∝ M/L ∝ M⁻²·⁵
  • L ∝ M³·⁵ (MS mass-luminosity)
  • Chandrasekhar limit: 1.4 M☉ — max WD core mass
  • NS upper limit: ~3 M☉ core → above = BH
  • Schwarzschild radius: R_s = 2GM/c² (where V_esc = c → event horizon)
Exam tip (from prof): You won't plug & chug numbers. Know how formulas WORK — "what happens to V_esc if you double R?" Answer: V_esc decreases by factor of √2.
Ch 17 — Star Stuff / Stellar Evolution (~35%) ← BIGGEST SECTION
LOW-MASS STARS (0.1–2 M☉):
1. Main Seq (H→He core, pp chain) → 2. Red Giant I (H shell burning, inert He core slowly contracting) → 3. Helium Flash → Horizontal Branch (He→C core via triple-alpha, H shell) → 4. Double-Shell Burning → thermal pulses → Planetary Nebula → 5. White Dwarf (C/O core)
  • Red Giant: H exhausted in core → core contracts → heats → H shell ignites → outer layers expand & cool → moves RIGHT on HR
  • Helium Flash: He ignites suddenly in degenerate core (only if M < ~2.25 M☉). Core NOT in thermal equilibrium → runaway fusion briefly
  • Horizontal Branch: He fusing in core + H in shell. Stable. Hotter & less luminous than red giant tip.
  • Double Shell: He & H shells alternate → thermal pulses → ejects outer layers → planetary nebula
  • White Dwarf: C/O core. Electron degeneracy pressure. No fusion. Slowly cools → eventually "black dwarf."
HIGH-MASS STARS (>8 M☉): From Lecture
1. Main Seq (H→He, CNO cycle) → 2. Red Giant I (H shell, inert He core) → 3. Horiz Branch (no He flash if M>2.25, smooth He core burn) → 4. Red Giant II / Supergiant (He shell + H shell, inert C core) → 5. Carbon flash → successive burning via alpha (He) capture: C→O→Ne→Si→Fe. "Onion ring" shells. → 6. Fe core → collapse <0.01 sec → SUPERNOVA → Neutron Star or Black Hole
  • Iron dead end: Fe = highest binding energy. Fusion ABSORBS energy → no thermal pressure. Electron deg. pressure supports core briefly until mass above too heavy.
  • Core collapse: p + e⁻ → n + ν. Entire core in <0.01s! Electron deg. pressure GONE.
  • Supernova: Neutron deg. pressure halts collapse abruptly → infalling atmosphere bounces off core + huge neutrino energy release → explosion. Disperses heavy elements through galaxy.
  • SN so energetic they produce elements heavier than Fe
  • CNO cycle: Faster H→He fusion using C,N,O as catalysts. Dominates in high-mass stars.
INTERMEDIATE-MASS (2–8 M☉):
  • "Starts like high-mass, ends like low-mass"
  • No helium flash (core not degenerate, M>2.25). No supernova. → planetary nebula → white dwarf.
Key Concept: At every stage ask: What's fusing? Where? Is gravity vs pressure in balance? No fusion → gravity wins → contraction → heating → next fuel? | Evidence: Even-numbered elements more abundant (He capture ✓). Elements beyond Fe rare (SN-made ✓). Ch 18 — Stellar Graveyard (~25%)
  • WHITE DWARF (star < 8M☉):
    • Gravity vs. electron degeneracy pressure
    • Core < 1.4 M☉ (Chandrasekhar limit)
    • Size of Earth, mass of Sun. No fusion.
    • Nova: WD in binary accretes H via accretion disk → surface H fuses only → brightens. Star survives! Repeatable.
    • Type Ia SN: WD accretes past 1.4 M☉ → electron deg. overcome → collapses → carbon fusion explodes entire WD. Nothing left! Standard candle.
  • NEUTRON STAR (star 8–20M☉):
    • Gravity vs. neutron degeneracy pressure
    • Core ~1–3 M☉. Size ~10 km (city-sized!)
    • Spins fast — conservation of angular momentum. Can rotate 100s–1000s×/sec!
    • Pulsar = rotating NS, beamed radiation, lighthouse effect. All pulsars are NS but NOT all NS are pulsars! (Crab Nebula pulsar = evidence)
    • X-ray binary: Accretion disk → continuous X-rays from hot disk
    • X-ray burster: Explosive He fusion on NS surface → X-ray burst
  • BLACK HOLE (star > 20M☉):
    • Core > 3 M☉ → gravity wins over everything
    • Collapses to singularity (infinitely small point)
    • Event horizon = where V_esc = c. NOT a hard surface — "distance of no return." Inside is outside observable universe.
  • ALGOL PARADOX:
    • Less massive star more evolved? → Mass transfer! Originally more massive star became RG, poured mass onto companion. Now 0.8 M☉ RG + 3.7 M☉ MS star.
Nova vs SN: Nova = surface H fusion on WD, survives, repeatable. Type Ia SN = WD destroyed (>1.4M☉, carbon detonation). Type II SN = core-collapse of massive star (>8M☉), leaves NS or BH.
S3 — Spacetime & Gravity / General Relativity (~5%)
  • Equivalence Principle (Central idea of GR): Effects of gravity and acceleration are equivalent — not merely similar! Person in closed room can't distinguish Earth's surface from accelerating at 1g in space. Einstein: "happiest thought of my life."
  • Elevator thought experiments (from lecture):
    • Elevator accelerates up → scale reads MORE (weight appears to increase)
    • Cable breaks, free fall → scale reads ZERO (why ISS astronauts feel weightless!)
    • Light beam crossing accelerating elevator → appears to curve downward from inside
  • Key logic chain: Light bends in accelerating frame + equivalence principle = light bends due to gravity!
  • 1919 Solar Eclipse: Star positions shifted near Sun (1.75 arcsec deflection) — confirmed GR prediction. Made Einstein world-famous.
  • Gravitational Lensing: Massive foreground object bends background light → multiple images, arcs, Einstein rings/crosses. Observed by HST & JWST.
  • Spacetime Curvature: Mass curves spacetime (rubber sheet analogy). Objects follow shortest path (geodesic). Gravity IS the curvature of spacetime, NOT a force at a distance. "Matter tells space how to curve, space tells matter how to move" — John Wheeler.
  • Gravitational redshift: Light climbing out of gravity well loses energy → shifts redder. Observed in Sun & Sirius B (white dwarf).
